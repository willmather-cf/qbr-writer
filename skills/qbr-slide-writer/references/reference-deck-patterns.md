# Reference Deck Patterns

Headline, slide type, and storytelling patterns taken from a strong Brand
Marketing QBR (FY26 Q1, Brand Advertising and theNET). Use them to choose the
slide type, mirror the reviewer's cadence, and keep the story in a familiar
order. All examples below use placeholder numbers and names; never copy
figures from the reference deck into new copy.

Where the reference deck and the skill's Quality Bar disagree, the Quality Bar
wins. The main gap: several reference headlines are topic labels ("Goal
Attainment", "Campaigns", "Impact of X"). Keep the slide type, but rewrite the
headline as a claim.

## Story order

The deck runs as a funnel from purpose to proof to detail. Mirror this order
when the operator is drafting a full section.

1. Cover and agenda. Program name, quarter, presenters.
2. Objectives and measurement. The framework the room judges you by: layer,
   objective, primary KPI. Define every acronym in a footnote.
3. What we ran. Campaigns and event activations, with media split.
4. Overall performance. Executive Summary, then Goal Attainment.
5. Launches and optimizations. One slide per change or test.
6. Studies. Brand lift or sentiment, with methodology in a sidebar.
7. Performance by campaign. One slide per campaign.
8. Performance by platform, country, and audience. The deep dive tables that
   summary slides point to with "Details here".
9. Second program, if any, repeating the same spine.
10. Get involved. Where to find the team, the calendar, the channel.

Section dividers carry a plain section name and no event label.

## Slide types

Name the slide type in the Visual field so the operator picks the right
layout in the reviewer's template.

### Executive Summary (two column: Highlights and Lowlights)

- Three bullets per column at most. Each bullet is one sentence: what
  happened, then why it mattered or what caused it.
- Every bullet that has a deep dive ends with "Details here", linked to that
  slide.
- Lowlights name the cause and the date plainly, and say what changes next.
  Pattern: "<System> was <what happened> on <date>; <effect> since."
- Headline: a claim that sums up the quarter, not "Executive Summary".

### Goal Attainment (KPI table plus metric boxes)

- Table columns: KPI, Goal, Actual (Q/Q), Attainment. One column group per
  campaign, then an aggregate.
- A row of four or five metric boxes underneath: big number on top, metric
  name below (for example "<number> / Impressions").
- Table header carries total spend with its Q/Q change in parentheses.
- Footnote any scope caveat, for example which media the totals include.

### Test (Hypothesis, Result, Recommendation)

- Headline pattern: "Test: <claim in plain words>". The reference's best
  example states the finding, not the setup ("Test: Adding a second channel
  improves performance").
- Body has exactly three labeled lines:
  - Hypothesis: "If <change>, then <expected outcome>."
  - Result: what happened, including what did not work.
  - Recommendation: one forward action naming the system or partner.
- A comparison table carries the numbers, with flight dates in the column
  headers.
- One short callout states the takeaway in a phrase (for example "Better
  together: <channel A> drives clicks, <channel B> drives cost efficiency").
- When the test was flawed, say so and call for a retest. Do not dress it up.

### Impact (chart plus one big stat)

- Use for a Launch or Migration whose effect shows in one number.
- One chart, one large stat callout (for example "<-X%>" with a label such as
  "Impressions needed to drive a web visit"), and one sentence that explains
  the cause.
- Rewrite "Impact of <thing>" headlines as the effect itself, for example
  "<Channel> cut impressions per visit <X%>".

### Campaign Performance (one per campaign)

- Top block, four lines: purpose, target audience, markets, media channels.
- Middle block: up to three bullets on what drove performance, cause first.
- KPI table: KPI, Goal, Actual (Q/Q).
- Metric boxes and creative thumbnails.

### Stats (four column KPI)

- Four big numbers, each with a label and a Q/Q change in parentheses.
- Any number that looks unusual gets an asterisk and a footnote, for example
  "*Return to trend after losing tracking last quarter."

### Study or Case Study (sidebar)

- Sidebar holds the methodology: format, keywords, countries.
- Main area holds one sentence of finding and one chart.

### Deep Dive Table (appendix style)

- One table per cut: platform, country, or audience.
- Consistent columns across cuts (Spend, Impressions, CPM, Clicks, CTR, QV,
  CPQV, FF, CPFF) with an Overall row.
- A one-line data caveat above the table when totals differ from the summary.

## Headline and copy conventions

- Footer on every content slide: "<Program> | <FYxxQx> QBR".
- Every headline number carries its comparison in parentheses: "(+X% Q/Q)".
- Ratios and rates are written as the reader would say them: "QV at 50% of
  spend rate".
- Section names are short nouns. Content slide headlines are claims.
- Acronyms are defined once, in a footnote on the measurement slide.
- Celebrate people and new capabilities as Highlights when they changed the
  quarter (for example a new hire or a new tool in testing).
- Close with a "Get Involved" slide: calendar, chat room, email, wiki.

## What not to copy from the reference

- Topic headlines ("Campaigns", "Goal Attainment", "Impact of X"). Rewrite as
  claims.
- En dashes in date ranges ("Jan–Mar"). Write "Jan to Mar".
- Hedged forecasts ("Q2 should benefit"). State the mechanism and the date it
  takes effect, or flag it.
- Emoji as sentiment markers. Use plain words.
